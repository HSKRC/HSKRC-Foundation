Mission & objectives

Dignity, practical support and accountable humanitarian action.

HSKRC Humanitarian Foundation is being developed as an Australia-based humanitarian initiative. Its initial humanitarian focus is Afghanistan, with a proposed model built around need, transparency, safeguarding and responsible stewardship.

Our mission

Support people facing hardship without losing sight of dignity.

Our proposed mission is to support vulnerable individuals and communities through practical, carefully scoped humanitarian assistance that responds to clearly identified needs.

We aim to combine direct assistance with transparent project design, documented expenditure, appropriate safeguards and public reporting so that humanitarian support can be understood and reviewed.

Core commitment: assistance should be needs-based, respectful, non-discriminatory and delivered in ways that protect the privacy, safety and dignity of people receiving support.
Proposed objectives

Six objectives for the foundation’s first stage.

01

Humanitarian relief

Develop small, clearly defined relief projects for food, winter needs and urgent household support where credible need has been identified.

02

Education support

Support access to learning through school supplies, learning materials and practical education assistance suited to local circumstances.

03

Essential needs

Provide targeted assistance for essential household needs using transparent eligibility criteria and documented delivery processes.

04

Community resilience

Prioritise approaches that help households and communities respond to hardship while avoiding unnecessary dependency.

05

Accountability

Design each project with a defined purpose, budget, approval process, evidence requirements and a public summary of delivery and expenditure.

06

Responsible partnerships

Work only with partners or local contacts that can be appropriately verified and that accept the foundation’s safeguarding and reporting expectations.

Operating principles

How decisions should be made.

Dignity first

People receiving assistance are not marketing material. Images, stories and personal information should be used only when appropriate and with meaningful consent.

Needs-based support

Assistance should be based on documented need and transparent criteria rather than personal influence, affiliation or favouritism.

Do no harm

Projects should consider foreseeable safety, privacy, safeguarding and security risks before delivery begins.

Financial separation

Humanitarian funds should be kept separate from unrelated HSKRC research activities and supported by clear records and approvals.

Evidence and reporting

Project claims should be supported by appropriate records such as budgets, receipts, delivery documentation and completion summaries.

Continuous review

Projects and policies should be reviewed as the initiative develops, with stronger controls introduced as scale and risk increase.
